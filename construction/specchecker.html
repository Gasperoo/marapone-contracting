What coordination actually costs

Nobody reads 400 pages against 90 sheets.

The project manual and the drawing set are both contract documents. They are written by different people, revised on different cycles, and issued together as though they agree. Checking one against the other is a job nobody has time for, so it is done in fragments — by the PM who happens to notice, or by the supplier who calls to ask which one to price.

The failure is never dramatic. It is a door schedule that shows an unrated leaf where the manual requires 90 minutes. The rating is stamped on a label at the factory, so an unrated door cannot be upgraded on site — it is replaced, and the lead time is measured in months.

The window closes at the purchase order. Until the material is ordered, a contradiction is a question. After it, it is a change order, a delay claim, or a wall that has to come back down.

Eleven ways two documents disagree.

Below specified minimum

Inside the app

Every screen, no mock-ups.

Everything below is the shipping application running one real audit: Northgate Community Centre, a City of Toronto facility at 4120 Northgate Boulevard — one project manual, an architectural set, a structural/mechanical/electrical set and Addendum 02, all issued for construction. Nothing is canned. The reader, the parsers and the cross-check engine are the ones your own documents go through.

01 — Dashboard

Orders to hold, not findings to admire.

The headline number is not the finding count. It is how many material orders should not be released today — because that is the only version of this question a project manager can act on before lunch.

  • ▸A written review that names the worst conflict, the divisions it concentrates in, and how many RFIs would clear it
  • ▸"Deal with these first" pulls the blocking findings to the top with both documents quoted inline
  • ▸Every finding carries what it costs to get wrong and one sentence on what to do about it
02 — The conflict matrix

The table that goes to the consultant.

One row per conflict: division, section, item, property, what the specification says, the clause it came from, what the drawing says, the sheet it came from, the kind of conflict, severity, confidence and the action. It exports to Excel unchanged, which is the form this argument is usually had in.

  • ▸Filter to blocking only, or include what you have already resolved
  • ▸Every row cites the section number and the sheet number — no row without both
  • ▸A version snapshot is written on every cross-check, so what the documents said at tender stays recoverable
03 — Coverage

On a findings list, silence looks exactly like agreement.

This is the screen most products would not ship. An audit can only report a conflict where both documents say something about the same property. Where the drawings are silent there is nothing to disagree with — so the app tells you its own coverage before you treat a low finding count as good news. On the demo: 26 properties specified, 62% had a drawing to compare against.

  • ▸Clearance is shown by package, not by division — nobody releases "Division 08", they release the door package
  • ▸Each package is HOLD or CLEAR, with the properties behind the call
  • ▸A setup checklist blocks the confident-looking empty result: an audit run against one document, or against drawings nobody told the app were drawings
04 — RFIs

One letter per section, quoting both documents.

The app does not decide which document governs — that is a question for the consultant of record, which is exactly why the recommended action is to ask them in writing. One click drafts the letter per section, addressed to the consultant, citing both documents and quoting the wording of each, with a response date.

  • ▸Draft for everything open, or only for what is blocking a material order
  • ▸Grouped by section so the consultant who owns it gets one letter, not eleven
  • ▸Editable before it goes out, and tracked from draft to issued to answered to closed
  • ▸Exports as an RFI package, a PDF audit report, or the whole conflict matrix as Excel

05 — It works out what each file is

Drop the files on the window — PDF, Word, Excel, or a scan. CSI section numbers and PART blocks mean a manual; sheet numbers, title blocks and schedules mean drawings. The guess is shown on screen and you can override it. Every document is copied into the project's own folder, because a finding that cites page 4 is worth nothing once that PDF has moved off somebody's desktop.

06 — It learns what you call noise

Mark any finding real or noise and the verdict persists. Mute a property outright and it stops being compared. The pattern of what conflicts on your projects — which divisions, which consultants, which properties — accumulates on your own machine and makes the second audit measurably quieter than the first.

Why the findings hold up

A misquoted clause in an RFI is not recoverable.

Text recognition misreads exactly the characters that matter here: 5/8 as 5/B, 90 as 9O. So every finding carries a confidence, and it is not decoration — it decides where the finding sits on the list and whether it is marked "verify".

Certain

Clean text layer, a numbered spec section, a PART 2 product clause. The strongest thing a document can say.

High

Clean text, or a tagged schedule row under a header the app understood.

Moderate

A general note — read correctly; its scope is what is uncertain — or an inferred subject.

Low

Recognised by text recognition from a scanned page. Shown, sorted below the certain ones, and marked verify — open the sheet before quoting it.

It does not decide who is right

Which document governs is a question for the consultant of record, not for software. SpecChecker's job is to find the disagreement, quote both sides accurately, and put the question in writing to the person who can answer it.

It reads text, not geometry

It does not scale dimensions off a plan or read anything stated graphically. It reads what the two documents say — clauses, schedules, general notes — and compares statements to statements.

A general clause applies broadly, on purpose

It cannot tell that door D-8 is an office door. A clause that names no tag applies to the assembly, so a general 45-minute requirement is compared against every door in the schedule. Some of those are worth confirming and some are not — the RFI asks the right question either way.

You overrule the engine

Every finding can be resolved, dismissed or escalated; every property can be muted; every extracted value edited. Those calls persist and compound across projects — and they never leave your machine.

The knowledge base

Twenty-seven properties, and you can add the twenty-eighth.

A spec clause and a drawing callout are only ever in conflict about a measurable property of a named assembly — a thickness, a rating, a strength, a grade. Everything the engine does reduces to pulling those out of both documents, putting them in the same units, and seeing whether they agree.

Fire & life safety

Fire-resistance ratings, sprinkler design density, hardware grade, and the exit-stair and corridor assemblies they attach to.

Envelope

Thermal resistance and RSI, U-values, SHGC, glazing make-up, air and vapour barrier requirements.

Structure

Concrete compressive strength, reinforcing steel grade, structural steel grade, compaction requirements.

Interiors

Gypsum board thickness and core type, partition assemblies, door material and hardware grade.

Mechanical

Airflow, external static pressure, filter efficiency (MERV), piping material, and the units they belong to.

Electrical

Short-circuit interrupting ratings, conductor and raceway material, panelboard and distribution equipment.

Commercial

Warranty terms — the clause that costs nothing at tender and is worth arguing about five years later.

Adding a property is data, not code

Every property is an entry in attribute_rules.json — its unit table, the patterns that recognise it, whether more is better, how much difference is a real difference, and one sentence on what it costs to get wrong. Add an entry and the engine checks it on the next run. The CSI spine is MasterFormat, so the taxonomy is the one your estimators already read; the Ontario layer is named fields you can swap.

From document issue to answered RFI

Five steps, and the last one is the point.

01

Create the project

Name, number, client, consultant of record, and the issue status of each document set. The consultant is who the RFIs get addressed to.

02

Drop in the documents

Manual, drawing set, addenda, exported schedules. Each file is classified by what it looks like; the guess is shown and can be overridden.

03

Run the cross-check

Both sides are read into the same shape — one property, of one assembly, in one canonical unit, with the page or sheet it came from — and compared.

04

Work the findings

Sorted by what stops a material order, not by how many there are. Resolve, dismiss or escalate — and read the Coverage tab before calling it clean.

05

Send the RFIs

This is the step that saves the money. One letter per section, citing both documents, asking which governs — while the answer is still free.

06

Keep the record

A version snapshot from every cross-check. What the documents said at tender, before three addenda landed, stays recoverable — on a claim, that is the whole argument.
