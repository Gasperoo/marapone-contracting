// Regions & rate packs

EVERY JURISDICTION
IS A DATA PACK.

Every rate, tax percentage, statutory holdback, permit fee and code citation lives in a JSON data pack — a plain text file that ships with your source code. None of it is compiled into the engine.

That means changing jurisdiction is a data edit, not a rebuild. Packs cover every Canadian province and territory, all 50 US states, and every EU country plus the UK and Ireland. Toronto/GTA is the deepest of them, because it's the market we know best and it sets the standard the rest are built to.

// How the knowledge is separated

The engine is jurisdiction-neutral.
The local knowledge is a named layer on top.

This isn't a claim about intent — it's how the files are laid out. ScopeGuard's scope knowledge base is indexed by CSI MasterFormat, the same taxonomy used across North America. Jurisdiction-specific content sits in separately named fields that can be replaced without touching the requirement itself — which is exactly how one pack becomes the next.

215

Scope requirements

Across 20 trades. Every one carries a CSI MasterFormat code — 21 divisions, from 01 General Requirements to 33 Utilities.

59

Ontario-specific references

The local_ref fields — O.Reg. 213/91, Toronto Ch. 591, excess soil rules. About a quarter of the base. Swappable line by line.

~73%

Portable as-is

Hoisting responsibility, submittal cycles, cut-and-cap, as-builts — these are disputed on every job in every country. They move with you unchanged.

// one requirement, as it ships

{
  "code": "01-020",
  "title": "Hoisting, cranage and material handling for own work",
  "csi": "01 54 00",           // portable — CSI MasterFormat
  "criticality": "high",
  "unit": "LS",
  "note": "The single most disputed item on GTA mid-rise…"  // edit per market
}

The requirement, its keywords, its criticality and its unit basis are jurisdiction-neutral. Only the note is local — and it's a string in a file you own. Retarget it to Vancouver, Chicago or Milan by editing one line.

// Anatomy of a jurisdiction pack

What actually changes between markets

Every pack carries the same sections, so the engine reads them identically wherever you point it — and every statutory number inside carries its citation, printed beside it in the output, so an estimator can verify it before relying on it. Here's the full shape of the file, with Toronto shown as the worked example.

Price catalogues are separate again

95 material items and 24 crew compositions sit in their own files, with a city cost index applied on top — material, labour, equipment and subcontract factored independently, because a remote job has near-baseline material cost and a large labour premium.

On top of that sits a user-override layer and an optional supplier feed. Your real buyout numbers beat our defaults without anyone editing the baseline.

Everything prints its source

Each statutory number carries a citation string that appears next to it in the estimate and the proposal PDF. The pack is marked verify_before_bid for a reason — these are costing inputs, not legal advice.

When you retune a pack, the citation travels with the number. Whoever reviews the bid can check it against the current source.

// Where each market actually stands

Same structure everywhere. Different depth.

The mechanism is real and in production, and the coverage is broad — but the packs are not all equally deep, and it would be misleading to present them as though they were. Toronto is the reference implementation. Here is what that means for everywhere else.

Reference depth

Toronto & GTA

The deep pack. HST and the new housing rebate, Construction Act holdback and prompt-payment timelines, Toronto building permit and examination fees, development charges, parkland dedication and community benefits charge, Toronto Green Standard v4, the green roof by-law, Ch. 591 noise hours, ravine and heritage constraints, excess soil under O.Reg. 406/19, fair wage policy, Tarion warranty, frost depth and winter productivity.

Plus a 24-city Ontario cost index with Toronto as the 1.000 baseline. This is what a jurisdiction pack looks like when it's finished.

Pack available

Other provinces

BC, Alberta, Québec and the rest share the structure exactly — different holdback rules, lien periods, sales tax treatment and code references, same fields. The National Building Code lineage means much of the code section carries across with the provincial amendments layered on top.

Québec is the one that diverges most: CCQ labour rules and French-language documents both have real consequences for the pack, and it is the province whose local-practice notes we most want feedback on.

Pack available

United States

The closest fit of the three, because the scope taxonomy is already CSI MasterFormat — the divisions and section numbers a US estimator already reads, unchanged. What differs is statutory, and it differs per state: retainage and mechanics-lien deadlines, IBC with local amendments, FICA/FUTA/SUTA and workers' comp in place of CPP/EI/WSIB, sales tax in place of HST, Miller Act thresholds for public work.

Imperial units are native already — the catalogue runs SF, LF and CY alongside metric.

Pack available

Italy & the EU

We have a Rome office and work in the Italian market, so this isn't theoretical for us. The Italian pack carries VAT with reverse charge, the prezzario regionale as the price baseline, SOA qualification categories, CCNL Edilizia labour costs, Eurocodes with the national annex, and the ritenuta a garanzia in place of statutory holdback.

Elsewhere in the EU the same pattern holds: national VAT, a local price book, Eurocodes plus annex. Documents in the working language are a model configuration question, not a code change.

Being precise about the claim

Breadth of coverage is not the same as equal depth, and we'd rather you heard that from us than found it out. Every jurisdiction has a pack with the same field structure, so the engine behaves identically wherever you point it. What varies is how much local detail sits inside — Toronto carries green-standard, noise by-law, ravine and heritage content that has no equivalent in a pack for a market with no such rules, and there are markets where the statutory sections are complete but the local-practice notes are thinner than Toronto's.

Two things stay true everywhere: every statutory number carries the citation it came from, so it can be checked against the current source before a bid goes out; and after handover you can edit any of it yourself. That's what having the source means. Tell us your jurisdiction on the call and we'll show you its actual pack rather than describing one.
