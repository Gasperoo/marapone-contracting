// Coverage, pack by pack

Where each pack is regional, and where it isn't

Breadth means different things depending on what is in the file. A tax table has to be right for one jurisdiction and is useless anywhere else; a scope checklist is argued over identically on every job in every country. Here is which is which.

Pack Regional reality How it is organised
Building Code Compliance Fully regional — code content differs by law One folder per jurisdiction: all Canadian provinces and territories, all 50 US states, all EU countries plus UK/Ireland
Assembly Property Schema Regional by classification standard, not by border Two trees, shipped side by side: CSI MasterFormat for North America, EN/Eurocode for Europe. Deliberately not merged into one "universal" schema — they are genuinely different standards
Estimating Rate Pack Fully regional and time-sensitive — the fastest-decaying data here Per-jurisdiction rate tables, sold as a dated edition rather than an evergreen file. Every statutory number carries its citation in the row beside it
Bid-Leveling Template Largely structural — trade packages barely vary by geography One template, plus a region-notes tab flagging where NA and EU tendering conventions diverge
Master Scope Checklist Largely structural — the same items are disputed everywhere One checklist indexed by CSI code, with the same region-notes layer rather than a rebuild per jurisdiction

On the rate tables specifically

Permit fees, development charges and VAT rates are the fastest-decaying numbers we hold, which is why that pack alone is sold as a dated edition. Every figure in it carries its source citation so you can check it before you rely on it. Treat them as costing inputs to verify against the current schedule — not as legal or tax advice. Where a jurisdiction's figures could not be brought current for this edition, it is marked as such in the file rather than published as though it were fresh.

// "Why isn't this just the AI tool?"

The five questions people ask first

Does this cover my jurisdiction?

Yes — the Building Code and Estimating packs include every Canadian province and territory, all 50 US states, and every EU country plus the UK and Ireland. If you build outside those regions, get in touch before buying rather than after.

What's the difference between a Data Pack and the AI tool it came from?

The Data Pack is the reference library — a checklist, schema or rate table. You still apply it to your own documents by hand. The AI tool reads your actual drawings, specs or bids and produces cited findings and draft letters in minutes. Same knowledge; the tool is who does the reading.

Why is the Rate Pack dated "2026 Edition" but the others aren't?

Permit fees, development charges and VAT rates change every year; code and scope checklists don't move nearly as fast. We release a new Rate Pack edition annually — buying this one doesn't include next year's update, so you always know exactly how current your numbers are. That is deliberate: a silent update would leave you unable to tell.

Can I upgrade later?

Yes — any Data Pack purchase counts toward the price of its matching AI tool if you upgrade within 60 days. There is no code to enter: reply to your receipt and we apply it. See eligible tools →

How do I receive it?

Checkout is handled by Stripe, in Canadian dollars, with tax calculated for your region at checkout. Packs that are finished download straight from the confirmation page and from the link in your receipt — no waiting and nobody in the loop. For any pack still being finished, the confirmation page says so plainly and it is emailed within 12 hours. We would rather tell you which one you are getting than promise a link that isn't ready.

What am I allowed to do with the files?

Use them on your own and your clients' work, and copy them into your own spreadsheets and scripts, for as long as you like. What you can't do is resell them or republish them as your own product. There is no seat count and no licence server.
